# Discussion

The central result of this benchmark is a target distinction. A directed graph
constructed from interaction records can be informative for the dyadic
footprints of those records without retaining the records themselves. The Enron
fidelity audit makes the distinction unusually sharp: its capped formal concepts
have high footprint agreement with the native-event projection but zero exact
tail/head matches with native event types. MathOverflow shows the same
non-identity with lower footprint overlap. A formal concept should therefore be
reported as a structural object derived from an aggregate relation, not as a
recovered email or question-answering group.

Within the stated random native-event panels, higher-order ERGM conditional
footprint scores substantially improve on the density reference. The paired
results make this claim stronger than a single pilot split: the `M4c::L1g −
M0::L1` PR-AUC difference is positive in every primary data-mechanism cell, with
narrow percentile intervals over the 30 frozen masks. The claim is nevertheless
narrow. It concerns variation induced by repeated masking of one fixed network
under a fixed candidate universe. It is not evidence for a population effect,
for common performance across networks, or for calibrated probabilities of
natural event occurrence.

The hard panels clarify why this qualification matters. A random negative
matched by arity can differ from the positive in actors, roles, and footprint
structure. A reversal or role-exchange negative preserves far more of that
structure. In Enron, the random-panel ERGM advantage is near the balanced
reference under MCAR hard panels, whereas MathOverflow retains some directional
separation. Role assignment also changes the ordering of heuristic components
between the two datasets. These contrasts are not a failure of the random-panel
benchmark. They show that footprint completion and role recovery answer
different questions and should not be combined into one score claim.

Several limitations are substantive. The Enron core is deliberately conservative
because it retains only complete events within an approved internal-custodian
mapping. The MathOverflow events depend on a fixed reconstruction policy. Both
primary benchmarks use 100-node cores and a cap of three for tail and head
arity. Candidate panels are balanced and constrained by the observed
projection. MNAR-out uses oracle full-data activity to construct a stress test,
so it is not an observational estimate of an unknown real missingness process.

The ERGM interpretation is also restricted. The displayed scores are MPLE-based
conditional pseudolikelihood quantities. The factorized footprint summaries do
not become joint event probabilities merely because multiple dyad scores are
combined. The Enron higher-order MCMC feasibility gate did not converge or mix
within the predeclared bounded policy, so this paper does not report an
MCMC-derived `L3` dependence result. The M4 parameter-plug-in joint calculation
is retained only as a labelled supplementary score-mechanics sensitivity.

The chronological analysis should be read with the same restraint. It documents
that history-only and contemporaneous conditional-score specifications are
conceptually different and records which small MPLE branches were numerically
available. It does not evaluate future-event forecasts. A full temporal paper
will instead use rolling origins, native-event targets, TERGM/conditional STERGM
specifications where justified, and relational hyperevent models as event-level
competitors.

## Conclusion

A directed missingness benchmark must specify what has gone missing. Dyadic
footprints, formal concepts, and recorded tail/head events are related but
non-interchangeable targets. On the two primary native-event cores, ERGM
conditional footprint scores rank random held-out event types well relative to
a density reference, but those gains do not collapse the distinction between
footprint completion and recovery of a directed group. Reporting random, hard,
and role-specific tasks separately makes that boundary empirical rather than
rhetorical. The resulting framework is reproducible, explicit about failed
feasibility and reproduction gates, and deliberately leaves forecasting to the
separate temporal event-prediction study.
