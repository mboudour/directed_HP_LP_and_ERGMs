# SNAM Manuscript Format and Length: Calibration from Recent Publisher Articles

## Bottom-line recommendation

For the proposed SNAM methods manuscript, plan **8,000–9,500 prose words of main text**, with an editorial target of **about 8,500–9,000 words**. This range is intended for the narrative prose from Introduction through Conclusion, **excluding** the abstract, reference list, table and figure captions/contents, declarations, acknowledgements, and appendices. It is a conservative, evidence-informed planning target rather than a journal rule.

The manuscript's **six main-text tables**, **two to three figures**, rigorous mathematical development, and bibliography should be designed as complementary scholarly apparatus—not as a reason to repeat their results verbatim in prose. Put detailed derivations, robustness material, extended proofs, and supplementary diagnostics in appendices or supplementary files where appropriate; retain the assumptions, model definition, identification/estimation logic, and interpretation necessary for a self-contained main article.

Use a **150–250-word abstract** (the stated journal-specific requirement). **No SNAM journal word limit is asserted here**: this note does not verify one and should not be read as evidence of a formal limit.

## What was measured

> **Scope:** a transparent convenience sample of ten recent Springer/SNAM records supplied for assessment. It is **not a journal-wide census**, a representative random sample, or a verified statement of editorial policy.

The usable body-word estimates were reconstructed from accessible publisher PDFs, generally from the Introduction through the Conclusion and with front matter, references, and identifiable display material excluded. They are necessarily approximate: PDF extraction can retain or interleave equations, captions, and table/figure material. Counts therefore calibrate order of magnitude and drafting scope, not a precise production word count.

## Descriptive calibration

| Measure | Result | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Records assessed | 10 | All were assessed as Original Articles rather than corrections. |
| **Valid body-word-count sample** | **9** | The 72-page record was excluded from word-count statistics because no defensible body-only count was available. |
| Mean | **8,585 words** | Arithmetic mean of the nine available approximate counts (unrounded mean: 8,584.6). |
| Median | **8,700 words** | Middle observation of the nine available approximate counts. |
| Range | **5,321–13,510 words** | A broad observed range; it should not be treated as a permitted range or formal policy. |

The proposed **8,000–9,500-word** main-text prose range deliberately centers on the sample's mean and median while avoiding reliance on the 13,510-word upper-tail example. Aiming around **8,500–9,000 words** leaves room to explain a rigorous methods contribution without padding the introduction, restating tables, or duplicating mathematical material in prose.

## Format and interpretation caveats

1. **Article type is not publication-stage uniformity.** Every record was assessed as an Original Article (not a correction), but several are Article in Press or accepted-manuscript versions. Springer indicates that such files may later be replaced or edited into the Version of Record. Their pagination, wording, display placement, and count may therefore differ from final published versions.
2. **The measures are approximate accessible-PDF counts.** Source extraction used differing PDF layouts and exclusion steps. Equations, captions, tables, hyphenated line breaks, and two-column order can affect the result despite documented cleaning.
3. **A body word count is not an all-inclusive manuscript count.** Abstract, tables, figure captions, references, appendix/supplement, author matter, and declarations were excluded or removed where feasible. This is why the recommendation is explicitly a **prose main-text** target.
4. **The one uncounted record is informative only as pagination.** Its publisher PDF was 72 pages, but its complex interleaving of body, displays, appendices, declarations, and references made an exact enough body-only count unsuitable; treating it as zero would be misleading, so it is excluded from the statistics.
5. **Do not infer a rule from a convenience sample.** The sampled records are recent and relevant calibration points, not the full SNAM archive, a cross-sectional distribution of all submissions, or a substitute for current author instructions and editor guidance.

## Practical drafting allocation

A planning allocation compatible with the target is approximately: **1,000–1,300 words** for problem framing and related work; **2,500–3,200 words** for methods and mathematical specification; **1,800–2,300 words** for data/design, experiments, and results interpretation; and **1,400–1,900 words** for discussion, limitations, and conclusion. These are flexible drafting budgets rather than section requirements. The main paper should point readers to the six tables and two to three figures, synthesize their implications, and avoid narrating every cell or visual detail.
