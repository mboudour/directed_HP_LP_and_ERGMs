# Verified political-network and Senate covariate sources

This record supports the political-covariate revision of the Network Science manuscript. It distinguishes sources that motivate the model from the public sources that provide reproducible 118th-Senate covariates. Accessed 2026-10-25 unless otherwise noted.

## ERGM and co-sponsorship interpretation

- **Cranmer, Skyler J., and Bruce A. Desmarais (2011).** “Inferential Network Analysis with Exponential Random Graph Models.” *Political Analysis* 19(1): 66–86. DOI: [10.1093/pan/mpq037](https://doi.org/10.1093/pan/mpq037). Publisher record: <https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-analysis/article/inferential-network-analysis-with-exponential-random-graph-models/DFE95910D307A3936A6548DDFB3D0777>. Its congressional application includes same-party, same-state, absolute first-dimension DW-NOMINATE distance, committee overlap, reciprocity, cyclic triples, and transitive triples. The full 108th-House specification does **not** find an independent same-party coefficient; the manuscript should use this source to motivate the joint treatment of political covariates and endogenous dependence, rather than claim a universal party effect.

- **Fowler, James H. (2006).** “Legislative cosponsorship networks in the US House and Senate.” *Social Networks* 28(4): 454–465. DOI: [10.1016/j.socnet.2005.11.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socnet.2005.11.003). Publisher record: <https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0378873305000730>. The article treats a directed tie as public support from cosponsor to sponsor and reports persistent reciprocal cosponsorship. It connects this pattern to relationship-building and reciprocal support, while explicitly leaving its mechanism unresolved (e.g., shared interests versus strategic trading). It supports qualified, non-causal language.

- **Kirkland, Justin H. (2011).** “The Relational Determinants of Legislative Outcomes: Strong and Weak Ties Between Legislators.” *The Journal of Politics* 73(3): 887–898. DOI: [10.1017/S0022381611000533](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0022381611000533). Publisher record: <https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1017/S0022381611000533>. It treats cosponsorship as a noisy relational measure; its results on weak/bridging ties and legislative outcomes are observational rather than causal.

- **Bratton, Kathleen A., and Stella M. Rouse (2011).** “Networks in the Legislative Arena: How Group Dynamics Affect Cosponsorship.” *Legislative Studies Quarterly* 36(3): 423–460. DOI: [10.1111/j.1939-9162.2011.00021.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1939-9162.2011.00021.x). Publisher record: <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1939-9162.2011.00021.x>. Its nine-state study reports heterogeneous associations with ideology, geography, committee service, demographics, party, reciprocity, and transitivity. Transfer claims to the present Senate data must remain descriptive and qualified.

The underlying Fowler, Kirkland, and Bratton–Rouse data were **not** verified as current public replication datasets. They are citations for methods and interpretation, not replacement data sources.

## Reproducible 118th-Senate covariates

- **Voteview member ideology.** Official data page: <https://voteview.com/data>; member-field documentation: <https://voteview.com/articles/data_help_members>; live member CSV: <https://voteview.com/static/data/out/members/HSall_members.csv>. The CSV records `bioguide_id` and `nominate_dim1`. It is mutable, so the workflow archives the downloaded copy with UTC timestamp and SHA-256. Citation: Lewis, Jeffrey B., Keith Poole, Howard Rosenthal, Adam Boche, Aaron Rudkin, and Luke Sonnet. *Voteview: Congressional Roll-Call Votes Database*. <https://voteview.com/>. Supporting data-platform citation: Boche et al. (2018), “The new Voteview.com,” *Public Choice* 176:17–32, DOI [10.1007/s11127-018-0546-0](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11127-018-0546-0).

- **Party and state roster.** `unitedstates/congress-legislators`: <https://github.com/unitedstates/congress-legislators>. The current source snapshot was acquired from its documented `gh-pages` JSON outputs and recorded by checksum in the repository. Its README specifies Bioguide as the primary member identifier, chronological terms, party, state, and separate caucus/party-affiliation fields. The project is CC0; reproducibility requires a pinned source snapshot and term-date selection. Reference: United States (unitedstates), *congress-legislators: Members of the United States Congress (1789–Present)*, <https://github.com/unitedstates/congress-legislators>.

## Adopted static-covariate policy

The static ERGM M5 model uses party at the beginning of each actor’s 118th-Congress service (2023-01-03 for incumbents), serving state, and `nominate_dim1`. This is a fixed actor attribute policy and does not treat party or ideology as time-varying covariates. Voteview is joined by a unique 118th-Senate Bioguide record; if a Bioguide ID has duplicate records, the exact roster ICPSR match is selected. An event-isolated actor without a 118th Voteview record uses a documented latest prior-Senate score solely to retain the fixed 105-actor universe. The generated metadata records all exceptions and checksums.
